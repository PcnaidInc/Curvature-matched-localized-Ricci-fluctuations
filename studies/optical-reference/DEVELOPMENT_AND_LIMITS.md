The beam is a mathematical infinitesimal optical congruence. Its source and initial optical conditions are specified, and its sampled mean-radius perturbation differs from the RMS of one resolved photon's path. Geometric optics requires a probe-wavelength hierarchy in a future physical interpretation. Affine-window widths in the benchmark are not an observer's proper-time widths. The flat comparison uses parallel beams; the Schwarzschild reference uses radial beams whose background area shrinks. These cases are deliberately distinct reference tests, not identical spacetimes.
