\section{Exact prepared state and field evolution}
In numerical units $M=1$, define
\begin{equation}
 r_*(r)=r+2\ln(1-r/2),\qquad h(r)=2/r-1.
\end{equation}
The dimensionless switching time is $T=2$, and the initial radius $r_p$ is the root in $(1.5,2)$ of $r_*(r_p)=r_*(1.5)-1$. The switch is
\begin{equation}
 s(\eta)=\begin{cases}0,&z\leq0,\\
 [1+\exp(1/z-1/(1-z))]^{-1},&0<z<1,\\
 1,&z\geq1,
 \end{cases}\qquad z=\eta/2.
 \label{eq:prep}
\end{equation}
The auxiliary history is fixed by
\begin{equation}
 r'=-h s,\qquad A=1-s+s h,\qquad m_{\rm aux}^2=1-s,
 \qquad r(0)=r_p.
\end{equation}
For $\eta<0$, $A=1$, $r=r_p$, and $m_{\rm aux}=1$. For $\eta\geq2$, the field is massless and the background is Schwarzschild. The symmetry $s(2-\eta)=1-s(\eta)$ implies $\int_0^2s\,\dd\eta=1$; consequently $r(2)=1.5$. In the final region,
\begin{equation}
 \eta(r)=2+r_*(r)-r_*(1.5).
\end{equation}
The production code integrates the background with DOP853 and dense output, retaining an analytic relation for independent checks. The auxiliary history defines a quantum state rather than a semiclassical solution of stellar collapse. No stress computed on this auxiliary geometry is used to claim a physical collapse history.

The mode $\chi_{\ell k}=r f_{\ell k}$ has initial conditions
\begin{equation}
 \chi(0)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\omega_p}},\qquad
 \chi'(0)=-i\omega_p\chi(0),\qquad
 \omega_p=\sqrt{k^2+\ell(\ell+1)/r_p^2+1}.
\end{equation}
The Wronskian is $\chi\chi^{*\prime}-\chi'\chi^*=i$. For the numerical width/phase method, let
\begin{equation}
 q=k^2+A[\ell(\ell+1)/r^2+m_{\rm aux}^2],\quad w=\sqrt q,\quad W=w+d,
 \quad\chi=\frac{e^{-i\vartheta}}{\sqrt{2W}}.
\end{equation}
Writing $\chi'/\chi=u-iW$ yields the real evolution system
\begin{align}
 u'&=2wd+d^2+r''/r-u^2,\\
 d'&=-2u(w+d)-q'/(2w),\\
 \vartheta'&=w+d.
\end{align}
The direct complex oscillator equation is integrated independently for representative checks. Global constant mode phases cancel from the covariance. Positivity and normalization of the width representation are checked; no negative-width or failed integrator result is treated as a physical mode.

\section{Sampling functions and physical support}
\subsection{Proper time}
For a monotonically inward comoving trajectory, define
\begin{equation}
 P(r)=2\arcsin\sqrt{r/2}-\sqrt{r(2-r)},\qquad
 \tau=P(r_c)-P(r).
\end{equation}
Thus $\tau$ increases inward and $\dd\tau=\sqrt A\,\dd\eta$. The normalized time weight is
\begin{equation}
 F_\sigma(\tau)=\frac{e^{-z^2}c(z)}{\sigma\mathcal N},\quad z=\tau/\sigma,\quad
 \mathcal N=\int_{-5}^{5}e^{-z^2}c(z)\dd z,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 c(z)=\begin{cases}1,&|z|\leq4,\\
 [1+\exp(-1/v+1/(1-v))]^{-1},&4<|z|<5,\ v=|z|-4,\\
 0,&|z|\geq5.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Gauss--Legendre quadrature on $z\in[-5,5]$ is multiplied by $e^{-z^2}c(z)$ and normalized. The complete endpoint support is checked through $P(r)$, not inferred from the first and last quadrature nodes. The numerical field background extends to $r/M=0.2$; the narrowest inner radius in any time support is $0.2071283198M$. Longitudinal Gaussian and angular heat-kernel tails are not compact, so the complete spacetime test function is not a compact spatial cell.

\subsection{Longitudinal and angular normalization}
The longitudinal Gaussian is $G(x)=e^{-x^2/b_x^2}/(\sqrt\pi b_x)$ with $b_x=b_c/\sqrt{A_c}$. Its transform is $\widehat G(q)=e^{-b_x^2q^2/4}$. The squared pair-amplitude weight is therefore $e^{-b_x^2q^2/2}$, not $e^{-b_x^2q^2/4}$. The two real-momentum sectors have $q=k+k'$ and $q=k-k'$.

The angular weight is a normalized spherical heat kernel. With the pole along the $z$ axis,
\begin{equation}
 H_\delta(\Omega)=\sum_J\sqrt{\frac{2J+1}{4\pi}}
 e^{-\delta^2J(J+1)/2}Y_{J0}(\Omega).
\end{equation}
Its integral is one. Its squared $L^2$ norm is $\sum_J(2J+1)e^{-\delta^2J(J+1)}/(4\pi)$, giving the effective-area convention in the main article. Rotating the pole does not change the covariance for the isotropic state and background. A finite $J_{\max}$ is a numerical truncation of a smooth kernel, not a replacement by a sharp cap.

The ratios fixed at the window center are
\begin{equation}
 \sigma/L_K=0.1132696163,\quad b_c/L_K=1.4158702040,
 \quad r_c\delta/L_K=0.8495221224\ \text{or}\ 1.6990442447.
\end{equation}
These center ratios do not make directional expansion rates identical, and they do not make each point in a finite window locally static. The uniform control has no finite localized angular width.

\section{Angular reduction and positive pair norms}
Use orthonormal complex scalar harmonics and the field-expansion normalization in the main article. The relevant Gaunt integral is
\begin{equation}
 \int\dd\Omega\,Y_{\ell m}Y_{p n}Y_{J0}
 =\sqrt{\frac{(2\ell+1)(2p+1)(2J+1)}{4\pi}}
 \begin{pmatrix}\ell&p&J\\0&0&0\end{pmatrix}
 \begin{pmatrix}\ell&p&J\\m&n&0\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Integration by parts on the unit sphere gives
\begin{equation}
 \int\dd\Omega\,Y_{J0}\nabla Y_{\ell m}\cdot\nabla Y_{pn}
 =\frac{\ell(\ell+1)+p(p+1)-J(J+1)}{2}
 \int\dd\Omega\,Y_{J0}Y_{\ell m}Y_{pn}.
\end{equation}
Magnetic-index orthogonality removes cross terms between distinct $J$. The surviving $3j$ symbols require $|\ell-p|\leq J\leq\ell+p$ and even $\ell+p+J$. Summing field pairs only for $\ell\leq p$ adds a factor $2-\delta_{\ell p}$. These identities and exact-symbolic versus numerical weights are checked independently; standard harmonic identities are not claimed as new results [main article, Ref.~DLMF].

For a normal-ordered quadratic operator $Q$, the part of $Q|0\rangle$ orthogonal to the vacuum consists of two-particle amplitudes. The connected covariance is their real inner product. This gives a positive semidefinite Gram matrix and explains why both source projections must use the same field pairs. Additive identity-valued terms drop out after centering, but they do not turn an unsmeared product into a well-defined observable. The prescribed spacetime filters and high-frequency convergence remain essential.

For an angular pair, set $L=\ell(\ell+1)$, $P=p(p+1)$ and define $D,X,Z$ as in the main article. For a momentum sign $s$, let
\begin{equation}
 M_0=D-\tfrac12(L+P)Z,\quad B_0=M_0+s kk'X,\quad a_J=\tfrac12J(J+1).
\end{equation}
The six stored real moments, including momentum quadrature and the longitudinal factor, are
\begin{align}
 v_0&=\langle|B_0|^2\rangle,&v_1&=\langle\operatorname{Re}(B_0Z^*)\rangle,
 &v_2&=\langle|Z|^2\rangle,\\
 v_3&=\langle|D|^2\rangle,&v_4&=\langle\operatorname{Re}(B_0D^*)\rangle,
 &v_5&=\langle\operatorname{Re}(ZD^*)\rangle.
\end{align}
Brackets here mean the numerical $k,k',s$ integral, not a quantum expectation value. Before the angular coefficient, the joint matrix is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{pmatrix}
 v_0+2a_Jv_1+a_J^2v_2 & v_4+a_Jv_5\\
 v_4+a_Jv_5 & v_3
 \end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Summing with the factor in the covariance-spectrum equation of the main article and the heat-kernel coefficient recovers the full covariance. The independent table audit uses this reduction on saved blocks rather than copying the final CSV.

\section{Reference calculation and gravitational projection}
\subsection{Reference is a control, not a replacement source}
At fixed $r_c,A_c$, define $\omega_{\ell k}=\sqrt{k^2+A_c\ell(\ell+1)/r_c^2}$. A mode pair carries proper-time frequency $(\omega_{\ell k}+\omega_{pk'})/\sqrt{A_c}$. The same normalized time kernel is transformed at this frequency, and the same longitudinal and angular kernels are used. Multiplication by $-\omega_\ell\omega_p/A_c$, $1/A_c$, or $1/r_c^2$ gives $D$, $X$, or $Z$, respectively, from the common product.

The compact-kernel Fourier transform is evaluated on a dense grid and interpolated. Direct transform and time-quadrature tests check that acceleration. Reference cutoffs are varied independently. The local reference is not fitted to the numerical interior. It uses another geometry and state; close agreement does not establish an exact additive separation into static noise and a dynamical excess. The derivative observables remain distinct from an undifferentiated massless $\ell=0$ vacuum two-point function with its infrared qualification.

\subsection{Units and nonzero normalization}
Numerical source variances have units $\hbar^2/M^8$. Let $D_c$ denote the sampled dimensionless background quantity
\begin{equation}
 D_c=\int \dd\tau\,F_\sigma(\tau)\frac{\sqrt{48}}{r(\tau)^3}.
\end{equation}
The values are $32.37400395$, $76.80368124$, and $259.43329339$ for $r_c/M=0.60,0.45,0.30$. The ratio for a source variance $C_{AA}$ is
\begin{equation}
 \chi_A=\epsilon\,\frac{8\pi\sqrt{C_{AA}}}{D_c},\qquad
 \epsilon=G\hbar/M^2.
\end{equation}
The covariance of the two Ricci projections is $(8\pi G)^2\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)C\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$; its cross entry has the opposite sign from the source trace/time covariance. The energy combination gives the observer-direction Einstein projection. No ratio is taken to the vanishing background Ricci scalar or to a vanishing quantum mean.

The equations are leading Einstein-order local projections on a Ricci-flat reference. They do not determine the full constrained, retarded metric solution, Weyl curvature, intrinsic gravitational fluctuations, or higher-order polarization. The frozen reference is not put into those equations as a separate self-consistent gravitational spacetime.

\subsection{Frozen acceptance plan}
The source's validation plan predates the new production analysis. It requires: variance and normalized matrix changes below $1\%$ under last field-angular and matched-angular momentum/time/tolerance comparisons; external $J$ tail below $0.1\%$; previous outer-window overlap within $1\%$; representative independent identities/residuals below $10^{-8}$; covariance positive semidefinite up to $10^{-12}$ relative roundoff; and successful hash/restart/corruption rejection. The supplementary energy projection is checked separately. A supplementary failure is not repaired by overwriting a pressure or covariance component.

The authoritative JSON contains 43 true gates. The largest final primary variance change is $2.5373856\times10^{-5}$ as a fraction, or $0.0025373856\%$. At the outer overlap the trace/time variance discrepancies are approximately $1.74\times10^{-11}$ and $1.06\times10^{-10}$. The minimum reported normalized covariance eigenvalue is $1.5033\times10^{-4}$, not a negative-roundoff case. These are tested numerical comparisons, not rigorous infinite-mode remainder bounds or total physical confidence intervals.

\subsection{Failures retained}
The pilot's largest variance discrepancy is $0.3047984$ as a fraction and is rejected. This is not the discrepancy of the accepted fine grid. Earlier interrupted work is preserved as historical reporting, not used as a substitute for the available arrays. Small implementation or output-handling failures recorded in the original release are not evidence of a physical instability. The portable histories have their own interpolation checks; they are not bit-for-bit replacements for dense time arrays or unrestricted new-window data.

Both the residual-relative comparison and the parent-scale comparison should be reported. Neither justifies altering production data or relaxing a physical acceptance threshold after seeing a preferred sign. \section{Interpretation boundaries and next discriminating test}
The increase in the narrow-family trace ratio is $7.943948$ as the center radius halves. A conditional short-distance scaling $\operatorname{SD}(T)\sim\hbar L_K^{-4}$ and $\sqrt K\sim L_K^{-2}$ would give a ratio proportional to $r^{-3}$ and a factor eight. This is a manuscript-stage dimensional explanation, not an independent production simulation, a proof of universality, or an exact extraction of a curvature-only signal. The matched reference supplies the quantitative check.

The principal result is for minimal coupling. An improved stress tensor at another curvature coupling changes both the trace and the nontrace source. The known conformal massless trace-noise null result is a useful control, but does not mean all stress fluctuations vanish. At fixed final Ricci-flat geometry a fixed-state operator comparison is distinct from re-preparing a state on the non-Ricci-flat auxiliary history. The planned test should separate these changes rather than combine them.
